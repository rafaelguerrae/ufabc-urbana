# Learning path: sources and supported claims

Verified on 2026-11-03 by reading each page (abstract or official page). Full texts of the papers were **not** read; claims are limited to what the abstract or page states. Lesson text lives in `apps/web/src/lib/learn.ts`.

| id | Source | What it supports | What it does NOT support |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `epa` | US EPA, [Learn About Heat Islands](https://www.epa.gov/heatislands/learn-about-heat-islands) | Urban areas are warmer than outlying areas because structures are concentrated and greenery is limited. Surface heat islands (roofs, roads absorb and emit more heat than most natural surfaces; strongest by day) differ from atmospheric ones (warmer air, vary less). Trees and vegetation cool by shade and by transpiration and evaporation. Conventional urban materials reflect less solar energy and absorb and emit more heat. | Does not use the words "evapotranspiração" or "albedo"; those definitions come from `usgs-et` and `nasa-albedo`. |
| `almeida2024` | Almeida, P. L. B.; Ribeiro, F. N. D. (2024). Análise Multivariada de Ilha de Calor Urbana na Região Metropolitana de São Paulo, Brasil. *Revista Brasileira de Meteorologia* 39. DOI 10.1590/0102-77863910045. [SciELO](https://www.scielo.br/j/rbmet/a/ZNQcD7ZspfQ4FKkzhHJZmHt/abstract/?lang=pt) | Urban regions are warmer than rural ones; in the RMSP the effect is intensified by rapid, disorderly urbanization; it is associated with land-use patterns and the lack of green space; k-means found four clusters, one with a stronger effect. | Abstract gives no correlation values. Uses weather-station air data, not satellite surface temperature. |
| `souza2017` | Souza, C. V. F.; Rangel, R. H. O.; Cataldi, M. (2017). Avaliação Numérica da Influência da Urbanização no Regime de Convecção e nos Padrões de Precipitação da Região Metropolitana de São Paulo. *Revista Brasileira de Meteorologia* 32(4). DOI 10.1590/0102-7786324001. [SciELO](https://www.scielo.br/j/rbmet/a/ktdXwJFPg3cS7CdJ7VHJ4tn/) | Urbanized regions can become heat islands, mainly due to the scarcity of green areas and the presence of civil materials that absorb and retain heat longer than natural ones. Introduction (citing Gamarra et al. 2014): few green areas and water surfaces reduce evaporation. | The paper's focus is convection and rain, not a measurement of vegetation cooling. |
| `souza2019` | Souza, C. M.; Leite, L. P.; Perini, P.; Karzmierczak, L. (2019). A vegetação urbana a serviço do conforto térmico: uma proposta para um bairro metropolitano de São Paulo, Brasil. *Labor e Engenho* 13, e019014. DOI 10.20396/labore.v13i0.8654619. [Unicamp](https://periodicos.sbu.unicamp.br/ojs/index.php/labore/article/view/8654619) | Authors conclude that urban tree planting is a functional strategy for thermal comfort; trees shade paved surfaces. | The abstract reports no temperature measurement; the temperature benefit is cited from other work. Do not quote a temperature drop. |
| `usgs-ndvi` | USGS, [Landsat Normalized Difference Vegetation Index](https://www.usgs.gov/landsat-missions/landsat-normalized-difference-vegetation-index) | NDVI quantifies vegetation greenness; it is (NIR − red) / (NIR + red); Landsat 8-9 use bands 5 and 4; values scale to −1 to 1. | Does not say what NDVI values count as "dense vegetation". No threshold may be claimed from it. |
| `usgs-et` | USGS (2015). [The EarthWord: Evapotranspiration](https://www.usgs.gov/news/earthword-evapotranspiration) | Evapotranspiration combines evaporation and transpiration and accounts for the water that moves from the Earth's surface to the atmosphere; transpiration is water leaving plants as vapor through stomata. | Does not say plants or evapotranspiration cool cities; that comes from `epa`. |
| `nasa-albedo` | NASA Earthdata, [Albedo](https://www.earthdata.nasa.gov/topics/land-surface/albedo) | Albedo is a measure of the percentage of sunlight a surface reflects. | Not specific to urban materials; "asphalt and concrete reflect less" rests on `epa`. |

## Interactive tasks (v2)
The path is now a set of guided map tasks (click a target, compare two points, find your own street). Task texts add no new scientific claims. Values shown come from `districts.geojson` and the rasters at runtime; targets are the hottest and greenest units by mean surface temperature and NDVI, shown as examples, not as proof of cause.
